                        JUDGING   CRITERIA   & GUIDELINES                           
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      1. Purpose of the Judging Framework                                           
                                                                                    
      This framework makes IMPACT X judging consistent, transparent, fair, and practical across all rooms and all six tracks. It
      evaluates whether a team solves a meaningful problem, creates measurable impact, builds a credible solution, has a
      realistic path to adoption and return on value, and can continue beyond the hackathon.
                                                                                    
      Core principle: IMPACT X rewards the strongest combination of impact, innovation, technology, feasibility, business
      value, and long-term potential, not the most complex technology or the most polished presentation alone.

      5. Detailed Judging Rubric                                                    
      5.1 Problem Definition & Relevance — 10 Points                                
      •  Problem is clear, specific, and significant.                               
      •  Target users/beneficiaries are understood.                                 
      •  Research, interviews, data, or credible assumptions support the problem.   
      •  Existing alternatives are understood where relevant.                       
      Judge test: Would this problem still matter if the hackathon did not exist?   
      5.2 Impact & Track/SDG Alignment — 15 Points                                  
      •  Potential positive impact is clear.                                        
      •  Strong alignment with the selected track and SDG.                          
      •  Reach and depth of impact are credible.                                    
      •  Impact KPIs are identified.                                                
      •  Team explains how outcomes will be measured.                               
      Judge test: Who benefits, how much, and how will the team prove the impact?   
      5.3 Innovation & Creativity — 10 Points                                       
      •  Originality or creative problem-solving.                                   
      •  Meaningful differentiation from alternatives.                              
      •  Novel or unusually effective approach.                                     
      •  Innovation may be technological, business-model, process, experience, or service innovation.
      Judge test: What is meaningfully different or better?

      5.4 Solution Quality & User Experience — 10 Points                            
      •  Solution directly addresses the problem.                                   
      •  User journey is intuitive.                                                 
      •  Design fits intended users.                                                
                                                                                    
      •  Accessibility is considered where relevant.                                
      •  Prototype demonstrates core value.                                         
                                                                                    
      Judge test: Can a real target user understand and use it?                     
      5.5 Technical Implementation — 15 Points                                      
                                                                                    
      •  Working functionality appropriate to hackathon scope.                      
      •  Technology choices fit the problem.                                        
      •  Architecture and implementation show competence.                           
      •  Core integrations/workflows function.                                      
      •  Relevant security, privacy, reliability, data, and scalability considerations are addressed.
                                                                                    
      •  Complexity is rewarded only when it creates value.                         
      Judge test: Does the technology genuinely enable the proposed value?          
                                                                                    
      5.6 Feasibility & Execution — 10 Points                                       
                                                                                    
      •  Realistic technical and operational path.                                  
      •  Dependencies, constraints, risks, and resources are understood.            
      •  Credible MVP/pilot roadmap.                                                
      •  Reasonable assumptions about deployment, partners, regulation, or infrastructure where relevant.
                                                                                    
      Judge test: If funded tomorrow, could this realistically move toward launch?  
                                                                                    
      5.7 ROI & Business Model — 15 Points                                          
      •  Clear value proposition.                                                   
      •  Clear customer, payer, beneficiary, or funding source.                     
      •  Credible revenue, savings, funding, adoption, or value model.              
                                                                                    
      •  Market opportunity and alternatives are understood.                        
      •  Reasonable cost/pricing/savings/return assumptions.                        
      •  ROI may be financial, social, environmental, or organizational depending on the track.
                                                                                    
      Judge test: Who pays or funds it, why, what does it cost, and what measurable value is created?
                                                                                    
      5.8 Sustainability & Continuity — 10 Points                                   
      •  Clear post-hackathon roadmap.                                              
      •  Maintenance and continued development plan.                                
      •  Scalable beyond initial pilot/prototype.                                   
      •  Credible funding, revenue, partnership, or institutional support.          
                                                                                    
      •  Clear next milestone and evidence of commitment.                           
      Judge test: What happens after IMPACT X ends?                                 
                                                                                    
      5.9 Presentation & Communication — 5 Points                                   
                                                                                    
      •  Clear structure and storytelling.                                          
      •  Problem, solution, demo, impact, ROI, and future are understandable.

      10. Round 1 — Parallel Judging                                                
      •  Each judge independently scores every assigned team out of 110.            
      •  Judges submit individual scores before discussing a team.                  
                                                                                    
      •  Official room score = arithmetic average of valid judge scores.            
      •  Teams are ranked within their room.                                        
      •  Recommended: top 3 teams from each room advance.                           
      •  If room sizes differ materially, organizers will decide the number of qualified teams of the room.
      •  Qualification rules must not change after Round 1 begins.                  
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      11. Room-to-Room Fairness                                                     
                                                                                    
      •  Same rubric, score sheet, timing, and presentation rules in every room.    
      •  Brief all judges together before judging starts.                           
      •  Use the same score anchors and definitions.                                
      •  Where practical, balance technical and business expertise across rooms.    
      •  Judge against the rubric, not against the previous team.                   
      •  Do not revise a score merely because a later team seems stronger.          
      •  Borderline qualification cases may receive a structured calibration review based on original scores and the rubric.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      12. Round 2 — Final                                                           
                                                                                    
      •  Finalists present again with the same 10-minute maximum.                   
      •  Final panel should ideally include technology, business, impact/SDG, industry, and Google-technology expertise.
      •  Final judges score independently before discussion.                        
      •  Final assessment should emphasize impact, technical credibility, ROI/value, feasibility, continuity, and scalability.
      •  Exactly three overall winners are selected.                                
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      13. Tie-Breaking Rules                                                        
                                                                                    
       Priority Tie-Break                                                           
         1   Higher Impact & Track/SDG Alignment score.                             
         2   Higher ROI & Business Model score.                                     
         3   Higher Technical Implementation score.                                 
         4   Higher Sustainability & Continuity score.                              
         5   Higher Feasibility & Execution score.                                  
         6   If still tied, final panel conducts a structured comparison using the same rubric and records the decision.
                                                                                    
      14. Conflict of Interest & Fairness                                           
      •  Judges must disclose relevant conflicts of interest.                       
                                                                                    
      •  A conflicted judge should not score the affected team where practical.     
      •  Judges must not coach, favor, penalize, or influence a team during judging.
      •  Claims should not receive full credit without supporting evidence or a credible plan.
      •  Presentation charisma must not compensate for weak impact or execution.    
      •  Technical complexity must not automatically mean a higher score.

      18. Judge Quick Checklist                                                     
                                                                                    
      •  What real problem is being solved?                                         
      •  Who benefits and what measurable impact results?                           
      •  Does the demonstrated solution work?                                       
      •  Is the technology appropriate and credible?                                
      •  What makes the approach innovative or better?                              
      •  Who pays, funds, adopts, or benefits—and why?                              
                                                                                    
      •  What is the expected ROI or measurable value?                              
      •  Can it realistically launch?                                               
      •  What happens after the hackathon?                                          
      •  Can it scale?                                                              
      •  Was Google technology used meaningfully?                                   
      •  Did the team communicate effectively within 10 minutes?

      19. Definition of an IMPACT X Winner                                          
      An IMPACT X winner is not simply the team with the best prototype. It is the team that most convincingly
      demonstrates that a real problem can be addressed through an innovative, technically credible, feasible, economically
      or socially valuable solution with a realistic path to sustainable impact beyond the hackathon.
